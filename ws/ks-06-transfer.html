移乗いじょう介助かいじょ：車くるまいすへ

Transfer to a Wheelchair — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Guide a resident to stand and move safely into a wheelchair, cueing and encouraging them step by step.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさん、車くるまいすに 移うつりましょうか。
Tanaka-san, shall we move to the wheelchair?
Why: 移[うつ]る = 'to move (from one seat to another)' — the exact verb for a transfer (移乗[いじょう]). 〜ましょうか invites the resident to do it WITH you, respecting their autonomy. Announce the move before you start.
Tanaka-san (resident)
はい、お願ねがいします。
Yes, please.
Why: The resident agrees and invites your help with お願[ねが]いします. Only transfer when they've agreed — consent first, always.
Hana (caregiver)
では、ゆっくり 立たちましょう。 わたしの 肩かたに つかまって ください。
Then let's stand up slowly. Please hold onto my shoulder.
Why: 立[た]ちましょう = 'let's stand up'. つかまる = 'to hold on / grab'; 肩[かた]につかまってください = 'please hold onto my shoulder' — giving them something safe to grip. ゆっくり keeps it safe. Give ONE clear cue at a time.
Tanaka-san (resident)
よいしょ。
(with effort) Up we go.
Why: よいしょ is the sound Japanese people make with physical effort (like 'heave-ho' / 'here we go'). Hearing it tells you the resident is putting in effort — a natural cue that the movement is happening.
Hana (caregiver)
そうです、上手じょうずですよ。 ゆっくりで 大丈夫だいじょうぶです。
That's it — you're doing great. Slowly is just fine.
Why: そうです = 'that's right'; 上手[じょうず]ですよ = 'you're good at it / doing well' — encouragement keeps the resident confident and cooperative. ゆっくりで大丈夫[だいじょうぶ] removes pressure. Praise every small success.
Hana (caregiver)
体からだを こちらに 向むけて ください。 はい、ゆっくり 座すわりましょう。
Please turn your body this way. Okay, let's sit down slowly.
Why: 向[む]ける = 'to turn (something) toward'; 体[からだ]をこちらに向[む]けてください = 'turn your body this way' — こちら ('this way', polite for こっち) points them toward the chair. Then 座[すわ]りましょう = 'let's sit down'. Guide the turn, then the sit — one step at a time.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ありがとう。 楽らくに なりました。
Thank you. That's more comfortable now.
Why: 楽[らく] = 'comfortable / at ease'; 楽[らく]になりました = 'it became comfortable' (楽[らく] + に + なる, 'to become ~'). A sign the transfer went well and the resident is settled.
Hana (caregiver)
よかったです。 むりを しないで くださいね。
I'm glad. Please don't overdo it.
Why: よかったです = 'I'm glad (that's good)'. むりをする = 'to overdo / push too hard'; むりをしないでください = 'please don't overexert yourself' — a caring close that protects the resident from straining. 〜ないでください again for a gentle warning.
