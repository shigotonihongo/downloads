レクリエーションの 声こえかけ

Recreation — Inviting Everyone to Join In — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Invite residents to a group activity warmly, include a hesitant resident without forcing, lead with simple cues, and praise everyone.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
皆みなさん、今いまから みんなで 体操たいそうを しましょう。
Everyone, let's all do some exercises now.
Why: 皆[みな]さん = 'everyone' (polite), the standard way to address a group of residents. みんなで = 'all together', building a shared, inclusive mood. 体操[たいそう] = light exercises/calisthenics. Leading with energy and a clear 'let's all' invites participation without singling anyone out.
Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさんも 一緒いっしょに やってみませんか。
Tanaka-san, won't you give it a try with us too?
Why: やってみる = 'give it a try' (〜てみる softens it), and 〜ませんか is an invitation, not an instruction. Adding も ('too') to her name includes her warmly. This is the key skill — you invite, you never command participation.
Tanaka-san (resident)
私わたしは いいわ。うまく できないから。
I'm okay, thanks. I can't do it well.
Why: 私[わたし]はいいわ ('I'm fine / no thanks') is a soft refusal. Residents often decline out of self-consciousness — うまくできない ('can't do it well'). Never push. Instead, lower the barrier so joining feels safe and optional (next line).
Hana (caregiver)
見みているだけでも 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。
It's perfectly fine even just to watch.
Why: This removes all pressure. 見[み]ているだけ = 'just watching'; 〜だけでも = 'even just 〜'. Telling a reluctant resident that watching is enough often leads them to join naturally, on their own terms — which protects their pride and their sense of choice (自己決定[じこけってい]).
Hana (caregiver)
では、右手みぎてを ゆっくり 上あげてください。
Okay then, please raise your right hand slowly.
Why: Clear, simple, one-step cues work best — 右手[みぎて] (right hand), 上[あ]げる (to raise), ゆっくり (slowly). Demonstrate as you say it. Simple language and a visible model let residents with different abilities all follow along.
Tanaka-san (resident)
あら、これなら できるわ。
Oh my, I can do this one.
Why: あら = a soft 'oh!' of mild surprise, common in older women's speech. これなら = 'if it's this / this one', showing she found it manageable. The low-pressure invitation worked — now reinforce it immediately with praise.
Hana (caregiver)
上手じょうずですね！ その調子ちょうしです。
Well done! Just like that.
Why: Praise sustains participation. 上手[じょうず]ですね = 'you're doing well'; その調子[ちょうし]です = 'that's the way / keep it up'. Keep the tone warm and adult — never childish. Genuine encouragement makes residents want to come back next time.
Hana (caregiver)
楽たのしかったですね。また 一緒いっしょに やりましょうね。
That was fun, wasn't it. Let's do it again together.
Why: A warm close that plants a happy memory and a reason to return. 楽[たの]しかった = 'it was fun' (past of 楽[たの]しい); また = 'again'. Ending on shared enjoyment turns a one-off activity into something residents look forward to — good for mood and cognition.

3 · Patterns to Reuse — 使える かたち

PatternMeaning & use
