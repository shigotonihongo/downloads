観察かんさつと 報告ほうこく：いつもと ちがう

Observation — Spot a Change, Describe It, Report It — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Notice a change in a resident during everyday care: see she is not herself, ask before you look, describe the skin you see by place and colour, compare it with yesterday, ask about pain and itching, and report where, what and since when to the nurse — then keep observing and record it.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさん、着替きがえを しましょうか。
Tanaka-san, shall we get you changed?
Why: Dressing is the one moment each day you see most of her skin. 観察かんさつ — observation — is not an extra task; it happens inside the care you already give.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ええ、お願ねがいするわ。
Yes, please.
Why: Her answer is already information. Is her voice the same as yesterday? Is she slower to reply? You are observing from the first word.
Hana (caregiver)
今日きょうは 少すこし 元気げんきが ないですね。
You seem a little low today.
Why: Observation starts with the whole person — face, voice, appetite — before the skin. Say what you notice gently; it invites her to tell you why.
Tanaka-san (resident)
昨日きのうは あまり 眠ねむれなかったのよ。
I didn't sleep very well last night.
Why: A bad night usually has a reason — pain, the toilet, worry. Keep it in mind. The next few minutes may explain it.
Hana (caregiver)
背中せなかを 見みても いいですか。
May I look at your back?
Why: Ask before you look, every time — it is her body. 〜ても いいですか asks permission, and it also tells her exactly what you are about to do.
Tanaka-san (resident)
どうぞ。
Go ahead.
Why: Permission given. Now look properly, in good light. A quick glance is how redness gets missed.
Hana (caregiver)
腰こしの ところが 赤あかく なって いますね。
The skin on your lower back has gone red.
Why: Say what you SEE, in plain words: the place (腰こし) and the colour (赤あかい). Not a guess about what it is.
Hana (caregiver)
昨日きのうは ありませんでした。いつもと ちがいますね。
It wasn't there yesterday. That's different from usual.
Why: THE RULE OF THIS EPISODE. Your best tool is knowing what she is USUALLY like. 'Not there yesterday' turns a red patch into a change, and a change is what gets reported.
Hana (caregiver)
痛いたみや かゆみは ありますか。
Is there any pain or itching?
Why: Now ask what only she can tell you. Colour you can see; pain and itching you can only ask about.
Tanaka-san (resident)
座すわると 少すこし 痛いたいわ。
It hurts a little when I sit.
Why: Listen for WHEN it hurts. Pain on sitting means her weight lands right on the red skin — and it may explain the bad night.
Tanaka-san (resident)
悪わるい 病気びょうきかしら。
Could it be something bad?
Why: She is frightened. Do not guess what it is — and do not brush it off either. Both are promises you cannot keep.
Hana (caregiver)
看護師かんごしに 見みて もらいましょう。
Let's have the nurse take a look.
Why: 〜て もらう means someone does something for you. The right person looks, and she hears that help is coming — without you pretending to know.
Tanaka-san (resident)
そうね。安心あんしんしたわ。
All right. That's a relief.
Why: A clear next step calms people more than comfort you cannot back up.
Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさんの 腰こしに 発赤ほっせきが あります。
(To the nurse) Tanaka-san has redness on her lower back.
Why: Report WHO, WHERE, WHAT. 発赤ほっせき is the chart word for redness — the same thing as 赤あかい, said the way the nurse writes it.
Hana (caregiver)
今朝けさからです。昨日きのうは ありませんでした。
Since this morning. It wasn't there yesterday.
Why: SINCE WHEN is the other half of the rule. 'New since yesterday' tells the nurse how fast it came — and that it needs looking at today.
Hana (caregiver)
座すわると 痛いたいそうです。熱感ねつかんは ありません。
She says it hurts when she sits. There's no warmth.
Why: 〜そうです passes on what SHE said, marked as hers. 熱感ねつかん is what you checked yourself with the back of your hand. Say both, and keep them apart.
Hana (caregiver)
明日あしたも 観察かんさつして、記録きろくします。
I'll observe it again tomorrow and record it.
Why: One look is a snapshot; 観察かんさつ is watching over days. Written down, the next shift can see whether it is fading or spreading.
