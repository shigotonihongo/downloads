認知症にんちしょうの 方かたとの 会話かいわ

Dementia Communication — Calm, Simple, Patient Responses — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Answer a resident with dementia who wants to go home or asks the same question again: accept what she says instead of correcting it, keep your sentences short, redirect gently, and stay beside her until she settles.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Tanaka-san (resident)
ねえ、そろそろ うちに 帰かえりますね。娘むすめが 待まって いるのよ。
Listen, I'll be going home soon. My daughter is waiting for me.
Why: 帰宅願望[きたくがんぼう] — wanting to go home — is one of the most common things you will hear on an afternoon shift. She is not lying and she is not testing you: for her, right now, this is true. 〜のよ is 女性語[じょせいご], an older woman explaining something to you.
Hana (caregiver)
そうですか。娘むすめさんが 待まって いらっしゃるんですね。
I see. Your daughter is waiting for you, is she.
Why: The whole lesson is in this line: repeat her words back instead of correcting them. いらっしゃる is 尊敬語[そんけいご] for いる — you are speaking respectfully about her family. 〜んですね says 'I have understood you', which is what she actually needs. Correcting her only starts an argument you cannot win, and she will keep the fear long after she has forgotten your sentence.
Tanaka-san (resident)
そうなの。何時なんじの バスかしら。
That's right. I wonder what time the bus is.
Why: かしら is a soft 'I wonder', 女性語[じょせいご]. She is asking for a fact you cannot give her — so do not answer the fact. Answer the worry underneath it: she wants to know she is not stranded and not forgotten.
Hana (caregiver)
ご心配しんぱいですね。つらいですね。
You're worried, aren't you. That must be hard.
Why: Name the feeling before you do anything else (つらいですね, Ep 15). Two short sentences — with dementia, long explanations are noise. Say one idea, then stop and let her hear it.
Hana (caregiver)
お茶ちゃを 飲のんでから、一緒いっしょに 考かんがえませんか。
Shall we have some tea first, and think about it together?
Why: This is redirection, and notice what it does NOT do: it never says no. 〜てから = 'after doing 〜', so going home is not refused, only placed after the tea. 一緒[いっしょ]に makes it something you do with her, not to her (Ep 9). Very often the coat comes off during the tea and the subject changes on its own.
Tanaka-san (resident)
じゃあ、少すこし いただくわ。
Well then, I'll have a little.
Why: いただく is the humble 'receive / have', and 〜わ is again 女性語[じょせいご]. The redirection landed. Do not now announce that she has forgotten about going home — just pour the tea.
Hana (caregiver)
ありがとうございます。ゆっくりで 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。無理むりには しませんよ。
Thank you. Take your time — that's fine. I won't push you.
Why: Hurry is what turns confusion into fear. ゆっくりで 大丈夫[だいじょうぶ]ですよ removes the clock, and 無理[むり]には しませんよ (Ep 14) tells her she keeps the right to say no. Both are short enough to be understood on a bad afternoon.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ねえ、さっきの こと、もう 一度いちど 聞きいても いい？
Say, can I ask you about that again?
Why: The same question, again. 〜ても いい？ is 'may I?' — she is asking permission, which means some part of her knows she has asked before. That is exactly why your face matters more than your answer here.
Hana (caregiver)
はい、何度なんどでも どうぞ。ゆっくり お話はなししますね。
Yes — as many times as you like. I'll go through it slowly.
Why: 何度[なんど]でも どうぞ = 'as many times as you like'. Say it warmly, the tenth time exactly as you said it the first — she cannot remember the question, but she will read your tone perfectly. お話[はな]しします is the humble 'I will explain'.
Hana (caregiver)
窓まどの そばに 座すわりませんか。落おち着つくまで、そばに いますね。
Shall we sit by the window? I'll stay beside you until you feel settled.
Why: A calm place and a familiar person do more than any sentence. 〜まで = 'until', so you are promising to stay for as long as it takes, not for a minute. Sitting down beside her also puts your eyes at her level, which reads as 'I am not leaving'.
Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさんの ペースで 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。娘むすめさんには、私わたしから ご連絡れんらくいたしますね。
You can go at your own pace, Tanaka-san. I'll contact your daughter myself.
Why: Close by handing the day back to her (〜さんの ペースで, Ep 14) and by taking the one job she was worried about off her hands. ご連絡[れんらく]いたします is humble 'I will contact them' (Ep 16) — and it is a promise you must actually keep.
