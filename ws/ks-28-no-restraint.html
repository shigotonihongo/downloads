身体しんたい拘束こうそくを しない 介護かいご：見守みまもり

Dignity, Not Restraint — Find the Reason, Watch Over Him — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Handle a resident who keeps standing up alone without restraining him: go to him and ask why, meet the reason, stay beside him instead of stopping him, tell him plainly he will not be strapped in, give him a way to call, and take the risk to the team rather than deciding alone.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださん、どうしましたか。
Yamada-san, what's the matter?
Why: He is halfway out of his chair. Walk over, get down to his eye level, and ask. A shout from across the room makes people stand up faster, not slower.
Yamada-san (resident)
トイレに 行いきたいんです。
I want to go to the toilet.
Why: There is almost always a reason. Toilet, thirst, pain, boredom — standing up is how he tells you. Find the reason and most of the 'risky behaviour' disappears.
Hana (caregiver)
そうでしたか。一緒いっしょに 行いきましょう。
I see. Let's go together.
Why: THE RULE OF THIS EPISODE, first half. Answer the reason, not the standing. He wanted the toilet, so the toilet is where you go — together.
Yamada-san (resident)
一人ひとりで 行いけますよ。
I can go on my own.
Why: He wants to do things for himself. That wish is not a problem to manage — it is his 尊厳そんげん, his dignity. Keep as much of it as safety allows.
Hana (caregiver)
立たつ ときだけ、そばに いますね。
I'll just stay beside you while you stand.
Why: Offer the smallest help that keeps him safe. He walks; you stay close enough to catch a stumble. That is 見守みまもり — watching over, not taking over.
Yamada-san (resident)
椅子いすに ベルトを しますか。
Are you going to put a belt on my chair?
Why: Back in his chair, he asks what he is afraid of. Residents hear what staff say in the corridor. Answer the fear directly — do not change the subject.
Hana (caregiver)
いいえ、ベルトは しません。
No, we won't use a belt.
Why: Say no, clearly. A belt, bed rails all the way round, a locked table — that is 身体しんたい拘束こうそく, physical restraint, and in Japanese care it is treated as abuse.
Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださんには 動うごく 権利けんりが ありますから。
Because you have the right to move.
Why: THE RULE, second half. Give the reason, adult to adult. Moving freely is his right, not a favour the staff grant when they have time.
Yamada-san (resident)
本当ほんとうですか。安心あんしんしました。
Really? That's a relief.
Why: Relief is the point. A frightened resident stands up more often, not less — fear is one of the reasons people try to get up alone.
Hana (caregiver)
立たちたい ときは、この ボタンを 押おして ください。
When you want to stand up, please press this button.
Why: The alternative to a belt has to be practical. Put the call button in his hand and show him once. Now he has a way to ask instead of a reason to try alone.
Yamada-san (resident)
わかりました。呼よびますね。
All right. I'll call you.
Why: He agrees, and he will use it — once. What happens the first time he presses decides whether he presses it again.
Hana (caregiver)
すぐ 来きますから、無理むりしないで くださいね。
I'll come right away, so please don't push yourself.
Why: A button only works if someone comes. Keep this promise: if he waits ten minutes once, next time he will stand up alone.
Hana (caregiver)
一人ひとりで 決きめずに、チームで 相談そうだんします。
I won't decide this alone — I'll talk it over with the team.
Why: Said to the shift leader. How to keep him safe is a TEAM decision, written down and reviewed. One tired caregiver never picks restraint, and never picks 'just this once'.
Hana (caregiver)
立たつ 理由りゆうは トイレでしたと 報告ほうこくします。
I'll report that he stood up because he needed the toilet.
Why: Report the REASON, not only 'he keeps standing up'. A reason can be planned for — a toilet round every two hours may end the standing altogether.
Hana (caregiver)
身体しんたい拘束こうそくでは なく、見守みまもりに します。
Not restraint — we'll watch over him instead.
Why: The line to bring to the team. 見守みまもり means more eyes and closer rounds, not more straps. It costs staff time, and it is the job.
