看取みとり：そばに いる

End-of-Life Care — Stay Close, Keep Talking to Her — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Support a resident at the end of life and her family: greet her even when she cannot answer, keep your voice low, invite the family to talk to her and sit close, say only what you have seen, stay with a grieving question instead of answering it, and report every change at once.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Daughter (visitor)
母ははは 聞きこえて いますか。
Can my mother hear us?
Why: The question families ask most. She cannot tell by looking, and she is afraid of saying the wrong thing in front of her mother.
Hana (caregiver)
聞きこえて いると 思おもいます。
I believe she can hear you.
Why: Care staff are taught that hearing is one of the last senses to fade. Say it gently, as what you believe, not as a medical promise.
Hana (caregiver)
お声こえを かけて ください。
Please talk to her.
Why: THE RULE OF THIS EPISODE, first half. Give the family something to DO. Many sit in silence for fear of upsetting her, and being told they may speak is often what they remember afterwards.
Daughter (visitor)
お母かあさん、来きたよ。
Mum, I'm here.
Why: She speaks to her mother the way she always has, in plain family speech. This is not your conversation — step back half a pace and let it happen.
Hana (caregiver)
どうぞ、そばに 座すわって ください。
Please, sit close to her.
Why: Bring the chair right up to the bed, near her head, where the daughter can hold her hand. Being close is most of what this care is.
Daughter (visitor)
母ははは 痛いたく ないですか。
Is my mother in pain?
Why: The fear under every other fear. Do not answer it from the doorway — go and look at her face and her breathing first.
Hana (caregiver)
今いまは 落おち着ついて いらっしゃいます。
Right now she seems settled.
Why: Say what you have SEEN — calm breathing, a relaxed face — and say 今いまは, right now. いらっしゃる because you are speaking to her family about her (Ep 11).
Hana (caregiver)
看護師かんごしにも 伝つたえますね。
I'll tell the nurse as well.
Why: A question about pain goes to the nurse every time, even when she looks calm. Say that you are doing it, so the daughter is not left wondering.
Daughter (visitor)
もう、だめなんでしょうか。
Is this the end, then?
Why: The hardest question you will be asked. You are not the one who answers it — the doctor and nurse speak about what comes next. Your job is to stay with her feeling.
Hana (caregiver)
つらいですね。
This is so hard.
Why: THE RULE, second half. Name the feeling and stop. Do not fill the silence with facts or cheer — a few quiet seconds beside her say more than any sentence.
Hana (caregiver)
私わたしも そばに いますね。
I'm here with you too.
Why: You are here for the daughter as much as for her mother. Stay a moment — then step out and give the family the room.
Hana (caregiver)
何なにか あったら、すぐ 呼よんで ください。
If anything changes, call me straight away.
Why: Put the call button in her hand. Breathing and colour can change quickly, and the family must never feel they are bothering you.
Daughter (visitor)
ありがとうございます。
Thank you.
Why: She is not thanking you for a task. She is thanking you for not leaving her alone with the question.
Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさん、娘むすめさんが いますよ。
Tanaka-san, your daughter is here.
Why: Before you leave, speak to Tanaka-san herself and tell her who is with her. Everything in this room is said TO her, never about her over her head.
Hana (caregiver)
今日きょうの 様子ようすは、すぐ 報告ほうこくします。
I'll report how she is today straight away.
Why: Breathing, colour, whether she swallowed, what the family asked — the nurse and the next shift need all of it. In these last days, small changes are the whole record.
