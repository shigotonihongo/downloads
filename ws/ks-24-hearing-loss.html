難聴なんちょう：前まえから ゆっくり 話はなす

Hearing Loss — Face Him, Speak Slowly, Check He Understood — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Talk with a resident who cannot hear well: come round to the front before speaking, ask if he can hear, slow down instead of getting louder, use his better ear, check understanding with a question he must answer, and report a change in his hearing.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださん、おはようございます。
Good morning, Yamada-san.
Why: He does not turn round. That silence is your first information: he did not hear you, and he does not know you are in the room yet.
Yamada-san (resident)
え？ 何なんですか。
Eh? What was that?
Why: He caught a sound, not the words. Do not repeat the same sentence from the same spot — change where you stand first.
Hana (caregiver)
前まえに 来きました。お顔かおを 見みて 話はなしますね。
I've come round to the front. I'll talk to you face to face.
Why: THE RULE OF THIS EPISODE, first half. A resident with poor hearing reads your lips and your face as much as he hears you. From the front, at his eye level, with light on your face.
Hana (caregiver)
聞きこえますか。
Can you hear me?
Why: Ask it plainly, once. It tells him you have noticed, and it tells you where to start — it is care, not a test.
Yamada-san (resident)
よく 聞きこえません。
I can't hear very well.
Why: 難聴なんちょう — hearing loss — is very common in older residents, and many are embarrassed by it. He told you honestly; answer in a way that makes it safe to keep telling you.
Hana (caregiver)
では、ゆっくり 話はなしますね。
Then I'll speak slowly.
Why: THE RULE, second half. Slower and a little lower, not louder. Shouting breaks the sound apart and makes a kind sentence sound angry — and the whole corridor hears his business.
Hana (caregiver)
右みぎと 左ひだり、どちらが 聞きこえますか。
Right or left — which ear hears better?
Why: Hearing is often better on one side. Ask once and remember it — then everyone on the shift can use his good side.
Yamada-san (resident)
右みぎの 耳みみが いいです。
My right ear is better.
Why: Now you know where to sit, where to stand at meals, and which side to speak from when you walk with him.
Hana (caregiver)
では、右みぎから 話はなしますね。
Then I'll speak from your right side.
Why: Move to his good side and stay in front of him. Say what you are doing, so the move itself does not surprise him.
Hana (caregiver)
今日きょうは お風呂ふろですよ。
Today is your bath day.
Why: One short sentence, one idea. A long sentence gives him more words to lose — give the key word, then stop and let it land.
Yamada-san (resident)
お風呂ふろですか。
The bath?
Why: He repeats the key word back. That is a good sign — he caught it — so build on it with the next short piece.
Hana (caregiver)
はい。10時じに 行いきましょう。
Yes. Let's go at ten.
Why: Numbers are the easiest thing to mishear. Say the time slowly, and hold up your fingers or point at the clock while you say it.
Hana (caregiver)
何時なんじに 行いきますか。
So what time are we going?
Why: Check with a question he has to ANSWER. People who cannot hear well learn to nod to be polite, so a nod proves nothing — the time said back to you does.
Yamada-san (resident)
10時じですね。
At ten, right?
Why: Now you know he has it. If he had said a different time, you would say it again — slowly, from the front — never louder.
Hana (caregiver)
そうです。大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。
That's right. You've got it.
Why: Confirm it warmly. A resident who is corrected all day stops trying to hear; one who is told he got it keeps trying.
Hana (caregiver)
分わからない ときは、また 聞きいてくださいね。
Whenever you don't catch something, just ask me again.
Why: Give him permission to ask. Many residents stay silent rather than ask twice — and silence is how a missed bath time or a missed pill happens.
Yamada-san (resident)
ありがとう。よく 分わかりました。
Thank you. I understood that well.
Why: Slower and from the front did what shouting never could. He heard every word — and he kept his dignity while you said them.
Hana (caregiver)
耳みみの ことは、すぐ 報告ほうこくします。
I'll report about your hearing straight away.
Why: A change in hearing can be earwax, an ear infection or a flat hearing-aid battery — all fixable. Tell the nurse today, and which ear is his good one.
