申もうし送おくり：看護師かんごしへの 報告ほうこく

Handover — Reporting to the Nurse — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Report a resident's condition and changes to a nurse clearly, and confirm instructions.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさんですが、今朝けさ 少すこし 熱ねつが ありました。
About Tanaka-san — she had a slight fever this morning.
Why: Start a report with 「[person]さんですが、…」 — the が here isn't 'but'; it's a soft lead-in that means 'regarding ~'. It flags the topic before the details, which is exactly how handovers open. 熱[ねつ]があります = 'has a fever' (literally 'a fever exists').
Nurse Sato
何度なんどでしたか。
What was the temperature?
Why: 何度[なんど] = 'how many degrees'. 度[ど] counts degrees (and also times/occurrences). でしたか is the polite past question ('was it?'). The nurse asks for the exact number — always report precise figures in care.
Hana (caregiver)
体温たいおんは 37度ど5分ぶでした。
Her temperature was 37.5.
Why: For body temperature the decimal is read as 分[ぶ] (ぶ): 37.5°C = 三十七度五分[さんじゅうななどごぶ] (さんじゅうななど・ごぶ). This is a fixed medical/care convention — know it, because you'll report temperatures constantly.
Nurse Sato
食事しょくじは とれましたか。
Was she able to eat?
Why: とれました = potential past of とる ('to take'); 食事[しょくじ]をとる = 'to have a meal'. Asking if she was ABLE to eat (potential) checks her condition gently. Topic marked with は (食事[しょくじ]は).
Hana (caregiver)
はい、半分はんぶんほど 召めし上あがりました。
Yes, she ate about half.
Why: 半分[はんぶん]ほど = 'about half' (ほど = 'approximately'). Even when reporting ABOUT the resident to a colleague, you use the honorific 召[め]し上[あ]がる for her eating — respect for the resident stays constant. Report amounts, not just yes/no.
Nurse Sato
わかりました。水分すいぶんを こまめに とってもらってください。
Understood. Please make sure she takes fluids frequently.
Why: こまめに = 'frequently / a little at a time' — a key care word for hydration. 〜てもらってください = 'please have (someone) do ~' — the nurse is instructing you to get the RESIDENT to drink. Dehydration (脱水[だっすい]) is a common risk, so this is a real instruction.
Hana (caregiver)
はい、承知しょうちしました。
Yes, understood.
Why: 承知[しょうち]しました is the humble, workplace way to say 'understood / certainly' — more professional than わかりました when receiving an instruction from a nurse or senior. Use this at work to sound reliable and respectful.
Nurse Sato
何なにか 変かわったことが あれば、すぐ 知しらせてください。
If anything changes, let me know right away.
Why: 変[か]わったこと = 'something that has changed'; 〜があれば = 'if there is ~' (conditional ば). 知[し]らせてください = 'please inform me'. This 'report any change immediately' instruction is the heart of 報連相[ほうれんそう] (report-contact-consult) — the backbone of Japanese workplace communication.
Hana (caregiver)
はい、承知しょうちしました。 よろしく お願ねがいします。
Yes, understood. Thank you — over to you.
Why: Hana closes the same way she answered the first instruction: 承知[しょうち]しました, not わかりました — to a nurse or senior, the humble form every time, not just once. よろしく お願[ねが]いします then hands the matter over ('please take it from here'); it is the standard way to end a handover in Japan.
