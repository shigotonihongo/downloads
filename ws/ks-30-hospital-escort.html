受診じゅしんの 同行どうこう：病院びょういんへ 付つき添そう

Hospital Visit — Prepare, Go With Him, Report Back — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Escort a resident to a hospital appointment: tell him when you leave and that you are going with him, check the insurance card and the nurse's medicine note, agree what he will tell the doctor, introduce yourself at reception, look after him while he waits, and report the visit to the nurse the same day with the doctor's letter.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださん、今日きょうは 病院びょういんの 日ひですね。
Yamada-san, today is your hospital day.
Why: Remind him first thing. A trip out is a big event for a resident, and some forget it or lie awake worrying. Naming it calmly lets him get ready.
Yamada-san (resident)
ああ、足あしを 見みて もらう 日ひだね。
Ah, the day they look at my leg.
Why: He knows why he is going. Good — a resident who understands the reason for the trip is calmer than one who is simply taken.
Hana (caregiver)
10時じに 出でます。私わたしが 付つき添そいます。
We leave at ten. I'll go with you.
Why: Tell him WHEN and WHO. 付つき添そう means to go along and stay at someone's side — you are not just the driver, you are with him the whole visit.
Yamada-san (resident)
一人ひとりじゃ 心配しんぱいだったから、よかった。
I was worried about going alone, so that's good.
Why: Many residents dread the hospital: a strange building, long waits, questions they cannot hear. A familiar face beside him is half of what the trip needs.
Hana (caregiver)
保険証ほけんしょうを 持もって いきますね。
I'll bring your health insurance card.
Why: No card, no appointment. Check the bag before you leave, not at the reception desk. 保険証ほけんしょう first, every time.
Hana (caregiver)
看護師かんごしから 薬くすりの メモも もらいました。
The nurse gave me a note of your medicines, too.
Why: The doctor will ask what he takes. A note from the nurse answers exactly; your memory might not. Never guess a medicine name.
Yamada-san (resident)
先生せんせいに うまく 話はなせるかな。
I wonder if I can explain it well to the doctor.
Why: He is nervous about the questions. Prepare with him BEFORE, in the calm of his room — not in a busy corridor with the doctor waiting.
Hana (caregiver)
夜よる、足あしが 痛いたいと 言いいましょう。
Let's tell them your leg hurts at night.
Why: Agree one or two points together. They are his words, said once in advance, so he can tell the doctor himself. You only fill in what he forgets.
Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださんの 付つき添そいで 来きました。
(At reception) I've come with Yamada-san, as his escort.
Why: At the reception desk, say who you are with and why you are there. Staff need to know you are the care worker, not family.
Yamada-san (resident)
待まつのは 疲つかれるなあ。
Waiting really tires me out.
Why: Hospital waits are long, on hard seats, in a noisy room. Tiredness makes older people confused and unsteady — watch him while you wait.
Hana (caregiver)
お茶ちゃを 飲のみますか。トイレは 大丈夫だいじょうぶですか。
Would you like some tea? Are you OK for the toilet?
Why: The waiting room is still care. Offer a drink and the toilet before he has to ask — in a strange building he does not know where anything is.
Hana (caregiver)
先生せんせいに 自分じぶんで 話はなせましたね。
You told the doctor yourself.
Why: After the doctor, notice what he managed. Speaking for himself is his dignity. Say it out loud — it makes the next visit easier for him.
Yamada-san (resident)
一緒いっしょに 来きて くれて、安心あんしんしたよ。
Having you come with me put my mind at ease.
Why: He noticed. For a resident, a hospital trip alone with strangers is frightening; with someone he knows, it is just an errand.
Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださんの 受診じゅしんを 報告ほうこくします。
(To the nurse) I'll report on Yamada-san's hospital visit.
Why: THE RULE OF THIS EPISODE. The trip is not over at the front door. Report the visit to the nurse the same day — a change can start tonight.
Hana (caregiver)
薬くすりが 一ひとつ 変かわりました。これが 手紙てがみです。
One medicine has changed. Here is the doctor's letter.
Why: Pass on the fact and hand over the paper — never your memory of it. A medicine change goes to the nurse, never straight into his cup.
Hana (caregiver)
同行どうこうの 記録きろくも 書かきます。
I'll write up the escort record, too.
Why: When you left, when you came back, what happened on the way. 同行どうこう is the word on the form for going with a resident.
