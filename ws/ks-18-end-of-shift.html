申もうし送おくりと 挨拶あいさつ：報連相ほうれんそう

End of Shift — Handover and Greetings to Staff — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Close your shift properly: greet the staff coming on, report what changed, pass on a message, invite them to ask you anything, and leave with the two set phrases every Japanese workplace expects.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
お疲つかれ様さまです。申もうし送おくりを お願ねがいします。
Thanks for your work. Let me hand over to you.
Why: お疲[つか]れ様[さま]です is how staff greet each other at work — not こんにちは. Say it every time you pass a colleague. It means roughly 'I see you are working'. Note the direction: 〜です to someone starting, 〜でした to someone finishing.
Suzuki-san (night staff)
はい、よろしく お願ねがいします。
Yes — go ahead, thank you.
Why: よろしく お願[ねが]いします (Ep 10) works before the work as well as after it. Here it means 'please go ahead, I am listening'. If you learn only two workplace phrases, learn this one and お疲[つか]れ様[さま]です.
Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさんですが、今日きょうは お元気げんきでした。
About Tanaka-san — she was in good spirits today.
Why: 「〜さんですが」 (Ep 10) opens a report: it puts the resident up as the topic and warns the listener that news is coming. Start with the good news when there is some — the night staff will work twelve hours on what you tell them now.
Hana (caregiver)
でも、夕方ゆうがたに 少すこし 咳せきが 出でました。
But she coughed a little in the early evening.
Why: This is 報告[ほうこく] — the report, the first of 報連相[ほうれんそう]. 咳[せき]が 出[で]る is the natural way to say a cough happened; it is not something she did on purpose. Say WHEN it happened (夕方[ゆうがた]): a change with no time on it cannot be tracked by the next shift.
Suzuki-san (night staff)
そうですか。看護師かんごしさんには 伝つたえましたか。
I see. Did you tell the nurse?
Why: Expect this question every time you report a change. The nurse is the medical decision-maker, so the first thing the next shift needs to know is whether she has already heard. 伝[つた]えましたか = 'did you pass it on?'
Hana (caregiver)
はい。佐藤さとうさんに 報告ほうこくして あります。
Yes. I've already reported it to Sato-san.
Why: 〜して あります means it is done and the result still stands — exactly what a handover needs. Name the person you told (佐藤[さとう]さん), never just 'the nurse': the next shift may need to ask her, and a report with no name in it is one nobody can follow up.
Suzuki-san (night staff)
承知しょうちしました。夜よるも 気きを つけて 見みますね。
Understood. I'll keep an eye on her tonight too.
Why: 承知[しょうち]しました (Ep 10) is the workplace 'understood' — warmer than わかりました when you are accepting a job. 気[き]を つけて 見[み]ます = 'I will watch carefully'. When you are the one receiving a handover, say this: the day staff need to hear that it landed.
Hana (caregiver)
それから、ご家族かぞくから お電話でんわが ありました。
Also, there was a phone call from the family.
Why: This is 連絡[れんらく] — contact, the second of 報連相[ほうれんそう]. A message is not yours to keep until tomorrow: pass it on within the same shift. それから is the small word that opens a second item, so the listener knows you are not finished.
Suzuki-san (night staff)
ありがとうございます。連絡れんらくノートを 確認かくにんします。
Thank you. I'll check the communication notebook.
Why: 連絡[れんらく]ノート is the shared notebook every facility keeps; the spoken handover and the written record must agree. 確認[かくにん]します = 'I will check' — a useful verb all day: 確認[かくにん]して きますね before you answer something you are not sure of.
Hana (caregiver)
分わからない ことが あったら、いつでも 相談そうだんしてくださいね。
If anything is unclear, please ask me any time.
Why: This is 相談[そうだん] — consult, the third of 報連相[ほうれんそう]. 〜たら is 'if / when', so you are opening a door rather than giving an order. Say it to the night staff, and believe it when it is said to you: asking early is the cheapest thing in care work.
Hana (caregiver)
では、お先さきに 失礼しつれいします。お疲つかれ様さまでした。
Well then, I'll be off. Thank you for your work.
Why: The two phrases that end a shift. お先[さき]に 失礼[しつれい]します — 'excuse me for leaving before you' — is said by the person going home, and お疲[つか]れ様[さま]でした, now in the past tense, to those who stay. Leaving without them is the one thing colleagues really do notice.
