機能きのう訓練くんれん：無理むりしないで

Functional Training — Invite, Count Together, Stop at Pain — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Support a resident through her daily exercises: invite instead of ordering, do the movement with her, count out loud together, praise each try, stop the moment she feels pain, and report exactly when the pain started to the nurse and the physio.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさん、機能きのう訓練くんれんの 時間じかんですよ。
Tanaka-san, it's time for your exercises.
Why: 機能訓練きのうくんれん is the exercise plan the physio wrote to keep her walking and standing. Announce it as a normal part of her day, in a warm voice — not as a chore on your list.
Tanaka-san (resident)
今日きょうは やりたく ないわ。
I don't want to do it today.
Why: Reluctance is normal — exercise is tiring and sometimes sore. Do not argue with the feeling. Your next line makes the task smaller, not louder.
Hana (caregiver)
少すこしだけ、一緒いっしょに やってみませんか。
How about trying just a little, together?
Why: THE RULE OF THIS EPISODE, first half. Invite, do not order. 少すこしだけ shrinks the task, 一緒いっしょに means she is not alone, and 〜てみませんか leaves her free to say no.
Tanaka-san (resident)
少すこしだけなら、いいわ。
Well, if it's just a little, all right.
Why: A small yes is still a yes. Accept it warmly and start at once, before the moment passes.
Hana (caregiver)
私わたしも 一緒いっしょに やりますね。
I'll do it with you.
Why: Sit facing her and do the movement yourself. Showing is clearer than any explanation, and it turns the exercise into something you share.
Hana (caregiver)
右足みぎあしを ゆっくり 伸のばして ください。
Slowly stretch out your right leg.
Why: One body part, one movement, one speed word. ゆっくり protects her joints and gives her time to follow — the same one-step cue as Ep 9.
Tanaka-san (resident)
こうですか。
Like this?
Why: She is checking she has it right. Answer straight away — a question left hanging makes people stop.
Hana (caregiver)
はい、上手じょうずですよ。
Yes, you're doing well.
Why: Praise every try, like an adult to an adult. Encouragement is part of the exercise, not a bonus after it.
Hana (caregiver)
一緒いっしょに 数かぞえましょう。いち、に、さん。
Let's count together. One, two, three.
Why: Counting out loud keeps her breathing. People hold their breath when they strain, and a held breath is how a gentle stretch becomes a dizzy one.
Hana (caregiver)
もう一回いっかい、できますか。
Can you do one more?
Why: ASK for the next one; do not announce it. できますか lets her judge her own body, and her answer tells you how tired she really is.
Tanaka-san (resident)
あ、膝ひざが 少すこし 痛いたいわ。
Oh, my knee hurts a little.
Why: The most important line in the episode. 'A little' is still pain. It is the signal the whole exercise was waiting for, not a complaint to talk her past.
Hana (caregiver)
では、すぐ やめましょう。
Then let's stop right away.
Why: THE RULE, second half. Pain means stop — now, mid-count, without finishing the set. Lower her leg gently with her and let it rest.
Hana (caregiver)
無理むりしないで ください。
Please don't push yourself.
Why: Say it so she knows stopping is right, not a failure. A resident who is scolded for pain learns to hide it — and hidden pain is how injuries happen.
Hana (caregiver)
どこが 痛いたいですか。
Where exactly does it hurt?
Why: Now find the place. どこが asks WHERE — a nurse needs 'right knee', not 'her leg'. Ep 26's どこか asked whether; this one asks which.
Tanaka-san (resident)
右みぎの 膝ひざです。もう 大丈夫だいじょうぶよ。
My right knee. It's fine now.
Why: She says it is fine, and it may be. It still gets reported. Pain that fades in a minute can come back worse tomorrow.
Hana (caregiver)
今日きょうは 二回にかい できましたね。
You managed it twice today.
Why: End on what she DID, with the number. Two stretches is a real result, and ending warmly is why she says yes tomorrow.
Hana (caregiver)
看護師かんごしと リハビリの 先生せんせいに 伝つたえますね。
I'll tell the nurse and the physio.
Why: The nurse checks the knee; the physio (リハビリの先生せんせい) decides whether the plan changes. You never change the plan yourself — you pass on what happened.
Hana (caregiver)
二回にかいで 膝ひざが 痛いたく なったと 報告ほうこくします。
I'll report her knee started hurting on the second stretch.
Why: Report WHEN the pain started, not only that it did. 'Right knee, on the second stretch' tells the physio exactly how far she can safely go.
