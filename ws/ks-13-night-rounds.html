夜勤やきんの 巡回じゅんかい

Night Rounds — Quiet Check-ins and a Call for Help — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Do a quiet night round, respond softly when a resident is awake, guide them safely to the toilet, and settle them back to sleep.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
（小声こごえで）田中たなかさん、失礼しつれいします。大丈夫だいじょうぶですか。
(quietly) Tanaka-san, excuse me. Are you all right?
Why: On night rounds you announce yourself 小声[こごえ]で ('in a low voice') so you don't startle the resident or wake others. 失礼[しつれい]します = 'excuse me / pardon the intrusion' — always say it before entering a room, day or night. Move quietly, keep the light dim.
Hana (caregiver)
お休やすみの ところ、すみません。どうされましたか。
Sorry to disturb your rest. What's the matter?
Why: お休[やす]みのところ = 'while you're resting' (お休[やす]み is honorific for sleep/rest), a courteous way to apologise for interrupting. どうされましたか uses される (尊敬語[そんけいご] of する) = 'what's the matter?'. Gentle, respectful — even at 3 a.m.
Tanaka-san (resident)
眠ねむれなくて…。トイレに 行いきたいの。
I can't sleep… I want to go to the toilet.
Why: 眠[ねむ]れない = 'can't sleep' (potential negative of 眠[ねむ]る). At night, needing the toilet is the most common reason residents are awake — identify the real need quickly and calmly. 〜たい = 'want to 〜' (行[い]きたい = want to go).
Hana (caregiver)
お手洗てあらいですね。ご案内あんないしますね。
The toilet, of course. I'll take you there.
Why: ご案内[あんない]します (humble 案内[あんない]する) = 'I'll guide/take you'. Night is when falls happen most — dark, drowsy, unsteady — so you escort the resident rather than let them go alone. Repeating the need back ('お手洗[てあら]いですね') confirms you understood.
Hana (caregiver)
足元あしもとに 気きをつけてくださいね。私わたしが そばに ついています。
Please watch your step. I'm right beside you.
Why: 足元[あしもと]に気[き]をつけて = 'watch your step / mind your feet' — essential at night. 私[わたし]がついています ('I'm with you') reassures. Keep a hand ready to support; guide toward the handrail. Calm, close supervision (見守[みまも]り) prevents the night falls that cause serious injury.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ありがとう。すっきりした。
Thank you. That's better.
Why: すっきりした = 'I feel relieved / better' (past of すっきりする). Once the need is met, gently settle the resident back to bed and reassure them to sleep — don't rush off. A calm handover back to sleep is part of the care.
Hana (caregiver)
ゆっくり お休やすみくださいね。何なにか あったら、この ボタンを 押おして 呼よんでくださいね。
Please sleep well. If anything happens, press this button and call me.
Why: お休[やす]みください uses 尊敬語[そんけいご] (お休[やす]みになる → お休[やす]みください) to gently wish them good rest. Then you point out the nurse call: 何[なに]かあったら ('if anything happens') 押[お]してください ('please press'). Leaving the call within reach means they can summon help instead of trying to get up alone.
Hana (caregiver)
また 見みに 来きますね。おやすみなさい。
I'll come to check on you again. Good night.
Why: 見[み]に来[き]ます = 'I'll come to see/check' (〜に 来[く]る = come in order to 〜) — telling them you'll be back is reassuring. おやすみなさい = 'good night', the full polite form. Then you record the round: time, that she woke, toileted, and settled — brief and factual.
