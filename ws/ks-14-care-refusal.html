When a Resident Refuses Care — Handling It Gently — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Accept a resident's refusal without forcing, find the reason, offer a small easy step, and re-invite gently — protecting trust and dignity.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさん、そろそろ お風呂ふろの 時間じかんですよ。
Tanaka-san, it's about time for your bath.
Why: A normal, gentle prompt — そろそろ ('about time now') 時間じかんですよ ('it's time'). But care must always be offered, never imposed, so when the resident refuses (next line) you respond with patience, not pressure.
Tanaka-san (resident)
いやよ。今日きょうは 入はいりたくない。
No. I don't want to bathe today.
Why: いや = 'no / I don't want to'; 入はいりたくない = 'don't want to get in' (〜たくない = don't want to). A refusal is a valid expression, not 'problem behaviour'. The first rule: STOP. Don't push, don't argue. Consent is the premise of all care.
Hana (caregiver)
そうですか。無理むりには しませんよ。大丈夫だいじょうぶです。
I see. I won't force you. It's okay.
Why: Accept the refusal immediately and out loud: 無理むりにはしません = 'I won't force it' (無理むりに = 'forcibly'). Forcing (無理強むりじい) is counter-productive — it deepens resistance and destroys trust (信頼関係しんらいかんけい). Removing the pressure first is what makes a later re-invite possible.
Hana (caregiver)
何なにか 気きになる ことが ありますか。
Is something bothering you?
Why: Now find the reason. 気きになること = 'something on your mind / bothering you'. Refusal usually has a cause — fear of cold, pain, embarrassment, 'I can do it myself', or confusion (認知症にんちしょう). Asking calmly (傾聴けいちょう = active listening) lets you solve the real problem.
Tanaka-san (resident)
寒さむいのが 嫌いやなのよ。
I just don't like being cold.
Why: The real reason appears: 寒さむい ('cold') 嫌いや ('dislike'). Now you can address it directly instead of pushing. This is why you always ask — the refusal wasn't about the bath, it was about the cold, which you can fix.
Hana (caregiver)
お部屋へやを 暖あたたかく しておきますね。先さきに お湯ゆに 足あしだけ つけてみませんか。
I'll warm up your room. How about starting with just your feet in the warm water?
Why: Solve the cause (暖あたたかくしておきます = 'I'll have it warmed up', 〜ておく = do in advance) AND offer a tiny first step: 足あしだけ ('just your feet') つけてみませんか ('shall we try dipping?'). A small, easy step lowers resistance — once they start, the rest often follows.
Tanaka-san (resident)
足あしだけ なら… いいわ。
If it's just my feet… all right.
Why: 足あしだけなら ('if it's just feet') いいわ ('okay') — the small step worked. Never celebrate too loudly or rush; keep it calm and let her keep control of the pace. If she'd still said no, you'd withdraw warmly and try again later — patience over pressure.
Hana (caregiver)
ありがとうございます。田中たなかさんの ペースで 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。また 見みに 来きますね。
Thank you. Your own pace is fine. I'll come and check on you again.
Why: Thank her (it's her choice, and she gave it) and hand back control: ペースで ('at your pace') 大丈夫だいじょうぶ ('is fine'). Respecting her pace preserves 自尊心じそんしん (self-esteem) and rebuilds trust for tomorrow. また 見みに 来きますね (Ep 13) closes warmly: no pressure now, and the door stays open.
