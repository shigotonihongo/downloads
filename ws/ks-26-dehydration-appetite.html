脱水だっすいと 食欲しょくよく：少すこしずつ 飲のむ

Dehydration & Appetite Loss — Offer, Don't Push, Report the Amount — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Notice the early signs of dehydration and appetite loss: see what was left on the tray, ask about pain, offer fluids little by little as an invitation he can refuse, let him choose the drink, praise the amount he takes, come back at a set time, and report the amount — not an impression.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださん、お昼ひるは 半分はんぶんでしたね。
Yamada-san, you had half your lunch today.
Why: Look at the tray before you say anything. Half a lunch left and the tea untouched is where dehydration starts in an older resident — notice it kindly, as a fact.
Yamada-san (resident)
あまり 食欲しょくよくが ないんです。
I don't have much appetite.
Why: 食欲しょくよく is appetite. Losing it can come from pain, a sore mouth, a fever or low mood. You do not guess which — you notice, ask, and pass it on.
Hana (caregiver)
どこか 痛いたいですか。
Does anything hurt?
Why: Ask before you offer food again. If something hurts, that changes what comes next — and it goes to the nurse first.
Yamada-san (resident)
いいえ。でも、少すこし だるいです。
No. But I feel a bit sluggish.
Why: だるい — heavy, tired, sluggish — is easy to wave away. Next to a poor appetite it is one of the quiet signs of 脱水だっすい, dehydration. Remember the word.
Hana (caregiver)
お茶ちゃは 飲のみましたか。
Have you had your tea?
Why: Check drinks, not only food. Look at the cup as well as asking — a full cup answers the question more honestly than a polite yes.
Yamada-san (resident)
いいえ、喉のどは 渇かわいて いません。
No, I'm not thirsty.
Why: The most common answer, and the reason dehydration is missed. Older people feel thirst less, so 'not thirsty' does not mean 'had enough'. Accept it, and still offer.
Hana (caregiver)
少すこしずつ 飲のみませんか。
How about drinking a little at a time?
Why: THE RULE OF THIS EPISODE, first half. Offer, do not push. 〜ませんか invites him and leaves him free to say no; small sips, often, do more than one big cup.
Yamada-san (resident)
冷つめたい お茶ちゃなら、飲のめます。
If it's cold tea, I can drink that.
Why: Now you know what he will take. Cold or warm, tea or water — the choice is his, and a drink he likes is a drink he finishes.
Hana (caregiver)
では、冷つめたい お茶ちゃを お持もちしますね。
Then I'll bring you some cold tea.
Why: お持もちします is the humble 'I'll bring it' (Ep 3). Follow his choice straight away — it shows him the choice was real.
Hana (caregiver)
一口ひとくちだけでも 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。
Even just one sip is fine.
Why: THE RULE, second half. Lower the bar. A resident told one sip counts will often take three; one told to finish the cup often takes none.
Yamada-san (resident)
ああ、おいしいです。
Ah, that's nice.
Why: Let him drink at his own pace, sitting upright (Ep 3). Do not refill the cup the moment he puts it down.
Hana (caregiver)
コップ 半分はんぶん 飲のめましたね。
You managed half a cup.
Why: Look at the cup and say the amount out loud, warmly. It praises him, and it fixes the number in your head for the report.
Yamada-san (resident)
もう 少すこし あとで 飲のみます。
I'll have a bit more later.
Why: He is setting his own pace, and that is fine. Your job is to make 'later' real — by coming back, not by hoping he remembers.
Hana (caregiver)
3時じに また 来きますね。
I'll come back at three.
Why: Name a time and keep it. Small drinks spread across the afternoon are how dehydration is prevented — and a promise kept is why he says yes next time.
Hana (caregiver)
だるい ことは、看護師かんごしに 伝つたえますね。
I'll tell the nurse that you feel sluggish.
Why: A poor appetite plus だるい goes to the nurse today, not tomorrow — it can be the start of a fever or dehydration. Say it calmly, so it does not frighten him.
Hana (caregiver)
お茶ちゃは 半分はんぶんと 報告ほうこくします。
I'll report it: half a cup of tea.
Why: Report an AMOUNT, never 'he drank some'. Half a lunch, half a cup, sluggish all afternoon — three facts the nurse can act on, written down the same way by every shift.
