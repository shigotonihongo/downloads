朝あさの 介助かいじょ：起床きしょう

Morning Care — Greeting & Waking a Resident — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Greet a resident respectfully in the morning, ask how they slept and feel, and gently help them get up.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさん、おはようございます。
Good morning, Tanaka-san.
Why: Always open with the resident's name + さん. It shows respect and helps a resident (especially with dementia) orient to who and where they are. Use the full おはようございます — the short おはよう is only for family and close friends, never for a resident you're caring for.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ああ、おはよう。
Oh, good morning.
Why: The resident answers casually (おはよう) — elderly people often speak plainly, and that is perfectly fine coming from them to you. You still keep the polite form. This one-way politeness (you polite, them casual) is normal and expected in care.
Hana (caregiver)
よく 眠ねむれましたか。
Did you sleep well?
Why: よく = 'well'. 眠れました is the past of 眠れる, the potential form ('to be able to sleep') — asking if they were *able* to sleep well is softer and more caring than the blunt 寝ましたか ('did you sleep?'). The 〜か at the end makes it a question. A sleep check is the standard, kind way to start morning care.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ええ、よく 眠ねむれました。
Yes, I slept well.
Why: ええ is a soft, warm 'yes' — gentler than はい and very common in speech. Notice the resident reuses the caregiver's exact phrase (よく眠れました). Echoing the question's words is a natural, easy way to answer in Japanese.
Hana (caregiver)
それは よかったです。気分きぶんは いかがですか。
That's good to hear. How are you feeling?
Why: それは よかったです = 'that's good (to hear)', using よかった, the past of いい — a warm way to react to good news. いかが is the polite form of どう ('how'), so 気分は いかがですか is a more respectful 'how do you feel?' than 気分は どうですか. Checking mood after sleep is a natural, caring follow-up.
Tanaka-san (resident)
大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。
I'm fine.
Why: 大丈夫 = 'okay / fine'. The final よ softly reassures you ('I'm fine, don't worry'). 大丈夫 is one of the most useful words in caregiving — you'll both ask it ('大丈夫ですか？') and hear it all day.
Hana (caregiver)
では、起おきましょうか。ゆっくりで 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。
Then, shall we get up? There's no rush — take your time.
Why: では = 'well then', signalling the next step. 起きましょうか uses 〜ましょうか = 'shall we 〜?' — you invite the resident to act *with* you rather than ordering them. Respecting their autonomy this way is the heart of good care (自立支援). ゆっくりで大丈夫 ('slowly is fine') removes pressure — kinder, and safer against falls.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ありがとう。
Thank you.
Why: The resident says the casual ありがとう. When YOU thank a resident, use the fuller ありがとうございます — again the one-way politeness of the care relationship.
Hana (caregiver)
お手伝てつだいしますね。
I'll help you, okay?
Why: お手伝いします is the humble form of 手伝う ('to help'): お + verb-stem + します lowers yourself politely while offering to serve — exactly right for staff helping a resident. The ね gently seeks connection and agreement ('I'll help you, alright?'), so it feels warm rather than pushy.
