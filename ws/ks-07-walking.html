歩行ほこうの 介助かいじょ

Walking Assistance — Walking Together Safely — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Invite a resident to walk, stand on their weaker side, set a matching pace with rhythm cues, and reassure and praise them — preventing falls.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさん、ゆっくり 立たって、少すこし 歩あるいてみませんか。
Tanaka-san, shall we stand up slowly and try walking a little?
Why: 歩[ある]いてみる = 'try walking' (〜てみる = 'try doing 〜'), which sounds gentler and less pressured than just '歩[ある]きましょう'. 少[すこ]し ('a little') keeps it small and achievable. Walking prevents 廃用症候群[はいようしょうこうぐん] (decline from disuse) and supports independence, but it must never feel forced.
Tanaka-san (resident)
そうね…、でも 転ころぶのが 怖こわいわ。
Well… but I'm afraid of falling.
Why: 転[ころ]ぶ = 'to fall (over)'; 怖[こわ]い = 'scared / afraid'. Fear of falling is extremely common and makes residents avoid moving — which weakens them further. Your job is to make them feel safe, so acknowledge the fear rather than brush it off.
Hana (caregiver)
私わたしが そばに いますから、安心あんしんしてくださいね。
I'm right beside you, so please don't worry.
Why: The reassurance that unlocks movement. 私[わたし]がそばにいます = 'I'm right beside you'; 〜から = 'so / because'; 安心[あんしん]してください = 'please feel at ease'. You stand on the resident's weaker (麻痺[まひ]) side, half a step behind — close enough to catch a stumble without getting in the way of the cane.
Hana (caregiver)
杖つえを こちらに ついてくださいね。ゆっくりで いいですよ。
Put your cane down here, okay. Slowly is fine.
Why: The cane (杖[つえ]) goes on the STRONG side and moves first: cane → weak leg → strong leg. つく here = 'to plant / set down' the cane. こちら ('this way / here') guides placement. ゆっくりでいい ('slowly is fine') removes any pressure to keep up — rushing is the main cause of falls.
Hana (caregiver)
いち、に、いち、に。その 調子ちょうしです。
One, two, one, two. That's the way.
Why: Counting a rhythm (いち、に = 'one, two') genuinely helps a hesitant or unsteady walker keep a steady gait — it's a real technique, not just encouragement. その調子[ちょうし]です = 'that's the way / keep it up', a warm nudge that builds confidence with every step.
Tanaka-san (resident)
なんだか 歩あるけそうだわ。
Somehow I feel like I can walk.
Why: 歩[ある]けそう = 'looks like I can walk' (potential 歩[ある]ける + そう 'seems'). なんだか = 'somehow'. This is the confidence your reassurance built — the emotional goal of walking care. Reinforce it right away with praise.
Hana (caregiver)
とても お上手じょうずですよ。あと 少すこしですよ。
You're doing really well. Just a little further.
Why: お上手[じょうず] (with honorific お) = 'skilful / doing well' — genuine praise sustains motivation. あと少[すこ]し = 'just a little more', giving a sense of the distance left so the goal feels reachable. Telling them how close they are keeps them going without pressure.
Hana (caregiver)
疲つかれたら 言いってくださいね。無理むりしないで、座すわって 休やすみましょう。
Tell me if you get tired. Don't push yourself — we'll sit down and rest.
Why: 疲[つか]れたら = 'if/when you get tired'; 言[い]ってください = 'please tell me'. 無理[むり]しないで = 'don't overdo it' — you always give the resident permission to stop. Pushing a tired resident risks a fall and breaks trust. Watch for ふらつき (unsteadiness) and be ready to support instantly. 座[すわ]って 休[やす]みましょう (座[すわ]る from Ep 6) names the safe exit: sitting, not stopping mid-corridor.
