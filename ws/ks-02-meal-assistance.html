食事しょくじの 介助かいじょ

Meal Assistance — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Announce mealtime, describe the food, encourage a resident to eat safely, and watch for choking.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
田中たなかさん、お昼ひるごはんの 時間じかんですよ。
Tanaka-san, it's lunchtime.
Why: お昼ごはん = 'lunch' — the お is a polite prefix that softens everyday nouns (you'll hear お茶, お水, お food words all day in care). 〜ですよ gently informs the resident. Always announce with their name so they orient to what's happening.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ああ、もう そんな 時間じかんですか。
Oh, is it that time already?
Why: もう = 'already'; そんな時間 = 'that (kind of) time'. A natural, mild surprise. Notice residents often keep 〜ですか (polite question) even when relaxed — don't worry about matching them exactly; you always stay polite.
Hana (caregiver)
今日きょうは お魚さかなですよ。
Today it's fish.
Why: Telling the resident the menu (お魚 = fish, with polite お) helps their appetite and orients them to the moment — a small but caring habit. 今日は = 'as for today', marking the topic with は.
Tanaka-san (resident)
おいしそうですね。
It looks delicious.
Why: おいしそう = 'looks delicious'. Attach 〜そう to an adjective stem (おいしい → おいし + そう) to say something *looks/seems* a certain way from appearance. The ね invites agreement.
Hana (caregiver)
ゆっくり 召めし上あがってください。
Please take your time and enjoy your meal.
Why: 召し上がる is the HONORIFIC (尊敬語) verb for 食べる/飲む ('to eat/drink'). Using an honorific for the RESIDENT's action raises them respectfully — exactly right in care. 召し上がってください = 'please eat'. ゆっくり ('slowly') keeps them safe and unhurried.
Tanaka-san (resident)
いただきます。
Thank you for the meal. (said before eating)
Why: いただきます is the fixed phrase said before eating — literally a humble 'I receive', expressing gratitude for the food. Everyone says it; encourage residents to as well. (After eating: ごちそうさまでした.)
Hana (caregiver)
お茶ちゃも どうぞ。むせないように 気きをつけてくださいね。
Here's some tea too. Please be careful not to choke.
Why: どうぞ = 'here you are / please go ahead' when offering something. The key care phrase: むせる = 'to choke/cough on'; 〜ないように = 'so as NOT to 〜'; 気をつけてください = 'please be careful'. Watching for choking (誤嚥 aspiration) is one of a caregiver's most important safety duties.
Tanaka-san (resident)
ありがとう。
Thank you.
Why: Casual ありがとう from the resident. You would reply with the fuller ありがとうございます — the one-way politeness of care again.
Hana (caregiver)
何なにか 手伝てつだいましょうか。
Shall I help you with anything?
Why: 何か = 'something / anything'. 手伝いましょうか uses 〜ましょうか = 'shall I 〜?' to OFFER to do something for them — polite and non-pushy. Offer help, but let them keep their independence (自立支援).
Tanaka-san (resident)
大丈夫だいじょうぶです。 自分じぶんで 食たべられます。
I'm okay. I can eat by myself.
Why: 自分で = 'by myself'; 食べられます is the potential form of 食べる ('can eat'). A resident asserting independence — respect it warmly. Encouraging what they CAN still do themselves is central to good care.
