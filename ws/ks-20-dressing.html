着替きがえ：麻痺まひの ある 手てから

Dressing Assistance — Undress the Strong Side, Dress the Weak Side First — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Help a resident change clothes: offer a real choice, take the sleeve off the stronger arm first, put it on the affected arm first, and check comfort before you leave.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださん、着替きがえを しましょうか。
Yamada-san, shall we get you changed?
Why: 〜しましょうか invites; 着替[きが]えを します is the everyday way to say it. Changing clothes means undressing someone, so it is asked, never announced.
Yamada-san (resident)
はい、お願ねがいします。
Yes, please.
Why: Wait for this before your hands move. A short yes is consent; silence is not.
Hana (caregiver)
どちらの 服ふくが いいですか。
Which clothes would you like?
Why: Hold up two and let him pick. In a facility almost nothing is chosen by the resident — clothing is one of the few places you can hand a choice back, and it costs you five seconds.
Yamada-san (resident)
白しろい ほうを お願ねがいします。
The white one, please.
Why: ほう marks one of two. When he answers, use exactly what he chose — a choice you then override is worse than never asking.
Hana (caregiver)
動うごく ほうの 手てから 脱ぬぎますね。
I'll take the sleeve off your stronger hand first.
Why: THE RULE OF THIS EPISODE, first half. Taking clothes OFF starts with the good arm, because the weak arm then slips out with no pulling. Say it out loud so he knows which arm to move.
Yamada-san (resident)
右みぎの 手ては あまり 動うごきません。
My right hand doesn't move much.
Why: He tells you the affected side himself. Take it as information you must act on, not as an apology — repeat it back so he hears that you heard it.
Hana (caregiver)
わかりました。ゆっくりで 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。
Understood. There's no rush.
Why: わかりました closes the loop on what he just told you. Then Ep 1's sentence does its usual work: a stiff shoulder tightens further when the person feels hurried.
Hana (caregiver)
着きるときは 麻痺まひの ある 手てからです。
When putting it on, we start with the weaker hand.
Why: THE RULE, second half — and it is the reverse of undressing. Dress the affected arm first while the garment is still loose; do the good arm first and the weak one has to be forced through a sleeve that no longer has any give.
Hana (caregiver)
そっと 袖そでに 手てを 入いれますね。
I'll slip your hand into the sleeve gently.
Why: Guide the sleeve onto the arm; never pull the arm into the sleeve. そっと tells him what your hands are about to do, which is what stops him bracing against you.
Hana (caregiver)
ボタンは 自分じぶんで できますか。
Can you do the buttons yourself?
Why: Ask before you take over. Anything he can still do, he should do — buttons are slow, and letting them be slow is the care, not a delay in it.
Yamada-san (resident)
ボタンは できますよ。
The buttons I can manage.
Why: He keeps one job. Stand back and let him finish it, even if your hands would be faster.
Hana (caregiver)
終おわりました。背中せなかを 確認かくにんしますね。
All done. I'll just check your back.
Why: A shirt bunched under the back presses on the skin all day, and that is where a pressure sore starts. Smooth it before you leave — the check takes a second and is part of the task.
Yamada-san (resident)
ありがとう。気持きもちが いいです。
Thank you. That feels good.
Why: 気持[きも]ちが いい is your confirmation that the clothes sit right. If it does not come, look again before you go.
