入所にゅうしょの ご案内あんない

Admitting a New Resident — Welcome, Orient, Reassure — Scenario Dialogue

Can-do: Welcome a new resident on their first day, show them their room and the call button, explain the daily routine, and reassure both them and their family.

1 · Dialogue & Why — 会話

Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださん、よく いらっしゃいました。本日ほんじつから ご入所にゅうしょですね。
Yamada-san, welcome. You move in with us today.
Why: First contact sets the tone for months. よく いらっしゃいました is the warm 'welcome / thank you for coming' (尊敬語[そんけいご] for 来[き]ました). ご入所[にゅうしょ] = 'admission / moving in' — the word every care home uses for this day.
Hana (caregiver)
介護かいごスタッフの ハナと 申もうします。
I'm Hana, one of the care staff.
Why: 申[もう]します is the humble form of 'my name is' (謙譲語[けんじょうご] for 言[い]う). Name yourself the moment you meet a new resident — a frightened newcomer needs to know who is looking after them before anything else happens.
Yamada-san (new resident)
はじめまして。少すこし 心配しんぱいです。
Nice to meet you. I'm a little anxious.
Why: 心配[しんぱい]です = 'I'm worried / anxious'. Almost every new resident says some version of this — a new bed, strangers, and no family in the building. Hear it as information, not as a complaint: it tells you exactly what your next sentence has to do.
Hana (caregiver)
ゆっくりで 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。私わたしが そばに いますから、安心あんしんしてくださいね。
Take it slowly — that's fine. I'll be right beside you.
Why: そうですよね accepts the feeling before answering it — never argue a worry away. Then two things a newcomer needs: no pressure (ゆっくりで 大丈夫[だいじょうぶ]、Ep 1) and a named person who is nearby (そばに いますから、Ep 7). 〜から = 'so / because', giving the REASON they can relax.
Hana (caregiver)
では、居室きょしつへ ご案内あんないしますね。お荷物にもつは 私わたしが お持もちします。
Well then, let me show you to your room. I'll carry your bag.
Why: 居室[きょしつ] = the resident's own room (the care-home word; お部屋[へや] is the friendly one you use with them). ご案内[あんない]します = 'I'll show you the way'. お持[も]ちします is humble 'I will carry it' — 尊敬語[そんけいご] and 謙譲語[けんじょうご] are how you show respect to someone older on a day that already feels undignified.
Hana (caregiver)
こちらが お部屋へやです。何なにか あったら、この ボタンを 押おして 呼よんでくださいね。
This is your room. If anything comes up, press this button to call me.
Why: Show the call button on the first day, before anything else in the room — it is the one thing that must not be discovered in an emergency. 何[なに]か あったら = 'if anything happens' (〜たら conditional). Point at it, and if you can, have them press it once so their hand knows where it is.
Yamada-san (new resident)
ありがとう。安心あんしんしました。毎日まいにちの 予定よていが 気きに なります。
Thank you. That's a relief. I'm wondering about the daily schedule.
Why: 安心[あんしん]しました = 'I felt relieved' (Ep 8) — the reassurance landed. 気[き]に なります = 'it's on my mind / I'm wondering about it' (Ep 14): a soft, indirect way of asking. Residents rarely demand information; catch the softly-asked question and answer it fully.
Hana (caregiver)
一日いちにちの 日課にっかを ご説明せつめいしますね。朝あさは 七時しちじに 起おきて、ご飯はんは 八時はちじです。
Let me explain the day. You get up at seven, breakfast at eight.
Why: 日課[にっか] = 'the daily routine'. Knowing the shape of the day is what turns a strange building into a place you live. Give the actual times, and keep it to two or three anchors — the rest can wait until tomorrow. 起[お]きて links the clauses (〜て form).
Hana (caregiver)
ご家族かぞくにも、何なにか ありましたら ご連絡れんらくいたします。どうぞ ご安心あんしんください。
We'll contact your family if anything happens. Please rest assured.
Why: Admission is two handovers at once — the resident and the family. ご連絡[れんらく]いたします (humble 'we will contact you', Ep 11) is the promise that lets a daughter go home, and ご安心[あんしん]ください is the set phrase for 'please rest assured'.
Hana (caregiver)
山田やまださんの ペースで 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。
You can go at your own pace, Yamada-san.
Why: Close the first day by handing it back to him. 〜さんの ペースで (Ep 14) says the home will move at his speed, not the other way round — the single most reassuring thing a new resident can hear, and the opposite of how an institution usually feels.
